Buying a yacht in Florida: a broker’s step-by-step checklist

From the first offer to the day you take the helm, these are the steps of a Florida yacht purchase and what to watch at each one.

By Garry Chupurdy Jr., licensed Florida yacht broker and captain · November 18, 2026

Every yacht deal is different, but the steps are the same. Here is the order I walk my clients through, and what I watch for at each step.

1. Make a written offer

The offer, usually on a standard purchase and sale agreement, sets the price and the rules for everything that follows: the deposit, the deadline to accept the boat after survey, the sea trial, and the closing date. Get these right at the start and the rest of the deal is much easier.

2. Put the deposit in escrow

A deposit of around 10% is common. It should go into a broker’s escrow account or a recognized escrow service, never straight to the seller. Make sure the agreement says exactly when it is refundable.

3. Survey and sea trial

Hire your own independent marine surveyor. Plan for a haul-out to see the bottom and running gear, and for an engine survey with oil analysis on larger boats. The sea trial should run the engines at full load, not just a lap around the harbor.

4. Accept, reject or renegotiate

After the survey you usually have three choices: accept the boat as is, reject it and get your deposit back, or ask for a price adjustment or repairs. A broker who understands the findings can tell you which items are real money and which are routine.

5. Check title, documentation and liens

Confirm the seller actually owns the boat and that it is free of liens. For Coast Guard documented vessels, review the abstract of title. For state-titled boats, check the title and any lienholder listed on it. A lien that is not cleared at closing becomes your problem.

6. Close

At closing, the balance is paid through escrow, the bill of sale and title or documentation papers are signed, and any lien is paid off. Florida sales tax is collected unless an exemption applies. (I wrote a separate guide on Florida boat sales tax.)

7. After closing

  • Title and register the boat, or file the change of ownership with the Coast Guard if she is documented.
  • Have insurance in place before you leave the dock.
  • Line up dockage, a captain if you need one, and a maintenance plan.
