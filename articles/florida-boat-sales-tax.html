Florida boat sales tax: the $18,000 cap and the nonresident exemption

How much sales tax you owe on a boat bought in Florida, why it never goes above $18,000, and how buyers from outside Florida can buy tax-exempt.

By Garry Chupurdy Jr., licensed Florida yacht broker and captain · November 18, 2026

Sales tax is one of the first questions every buyer asks me. Florida has two rules that make it one of the best states to buy a yacht in: a cap on the tax, and an exemption for buyers who live elsewhere.

The basic rate

Florida charges 6% state sales tax on a boat, plus any county discretionary surtax. Tax is figured on the net price, meaning the purchase price minus any trade-in.

The $18,000 cap

Since July 1, 2010, the most you can owe on the sale of a single boat is $18,000, and that cap includes the county surtax. In practice, the cap starts to matter at a purchase price of about $300,000. A $1 million yacht pays the same $18,000 as a $300,000 one.

The nonresident exemption

If you are not a Florida resident and you buy a boat through a registered Florida dealer or broker, the sale can be exempt from Florida sales tax, as long as you take the boat out of Florida. The main rules:

  • You sign an affidavit at closing stating that you qualify and will send the state the required documents.
  • The broker gives you a decal that lets the boat stay in Florida waters for up to 90 days after purchase. A second 90-day decal can extend that to 180 days.
  • After the boat leaves, you must send the Florida Department of Revenue proof that it left, and later proof that it was registered, titled or documented elsewhere.

Miss the paperwork or keep the boat in Florida too long, and the state can assess the tax plus a penalty equal to the tax. This is one place where having a broker who has done it many times really pays.

Buying a Coast Guard documented yacht

Documentation with the Coast Guard does not by itself exempt a boat from sales tax. If the sale happens in Florida and no exemption applies, tax is due.

This is general information, not tax or legal advice. Rules change, so confirm your situation with the Florida Department of Revenue or a tax professional. Sources: Florida Department of Revenue, Tax Information Publication 10A01-07 and its guide for boat dealers and brokers (GT-800006).
