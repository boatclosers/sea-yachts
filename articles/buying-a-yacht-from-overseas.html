Buying a yacht in Florida from overseas: how exports work

Florida has one of the largest used-yacht markets in the world. Here is how buyers abroad find, inspect, buy and export a boat without flying back and forth.

By Garry Chupurdy Jr., licensed Florida yacht broker and captain · November 18, 2026

A large share of my clients never set foot on the boat until after closing. Florida’s used-yacht market is deep, and buyers from Latin America, the Caribbean and Europe come here for selection and price. Here is how an export purchase works.

1. Narrow the search remotely

Your broker should be your eyes on the dock. I walk boats on video calls, open the engine room, and send photos of anything that matters before you spend money on a trip or a survey.

2. Independent survey and sea trial

Hire your own surveyor, not one suggested by the seller. You can attend by video, and your broker should be aboard for the sea trial to represent you.

3. Escrow and closing from abroad

Funds go into escrow, never directly to the seller. Closing documents can usually be signed and notarized where you live. If the boat will leave the United States, the paperwork should say so from the start, since that affects sales tax.

4. Export paperwork

  • Electronic Export Information (EEI). Most exports valued at $2,500 or more must be reported through the U.S. Automated Export System (AES) before the boat leaves. A licensed freight forwarder or customs broker normally files it.
  • Coast Guard documentation. If the boat is U.S. documented and you will register her in another country, the U.S. documentation is deleted so she can be flagged in your country.
  • Your country’s import rules. Duties, taxes and registration rules vary widely. Talk to an agent at home early, so there are no surprises when she arrives.

5. Getting her home

There are two options. You can run her on her own bottom with a delivery captain, which works well for the Bahamas and the Caribbean. Or you can ship her on a yacht transport vessel or cargo ship, which is common for longer distances.
