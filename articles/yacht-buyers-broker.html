Buying a yacht

What a yacht buyer’s broker does, and why it costs you nothing

The listing broker works for the seller. Here is what a buyer's broker does for you, how they are paid, and the questions to ask before you hire one.

By Garry Chupurdy Jr., licensed Florida yacht broker and captain · November 18, 2026

When you call the number on a yacht listing, you are calling the seller’s broker. Their job is to get the best price and terms for the seller. That is not a criticism. It is simply who they work for.

A buyer’s broker works for you. I’ve represented yacht buyers since 2008, and this is what that job actually involves.

What a buyer’s broker does

  1. Finds the right boats. Not just what is listed online, but what fits how you will use her: range, crew, draft, maintenance and resale.
  2. Looks at the boat like a captain. Before you spend money on a survey, a good buyer’s broker will tell you what they see in the engine room, the bilge and the maintenance records.
  3. Negotiates price and terms. The offer is more than a number. Deposit, survey period, sea trial conditions, what stays aboard and the closing date all matter.
  4. Manages survey and sea trial. When the surveyor finds something, someone needs to know what it means and what it is worth. That is where many deals are won or lost.
  5. Gets the paperwork right. Title or Coast Guard documentation, lien searches, sales tax, escrow and the closing documents. Most deals that go wrong go wrong here.

Why it usually costs the buyer nothing

In a typical brokerage sale, the seller pays the commission when the boat is listed. When a buyer’s broker brings the buyer, the listing broker shares that commission with them. The buyer does not write a separate check for representation.

That means the asking price is the same whether you have your own broker or not. The only difference is whether someone at the table is working for you.

Questions to ask before you hire one

  • Are you licensed? In Florida, yacht and ship brokers are licensed by the state. Ask for the license number and look it up.
  • Have you run and maintained boats like this yourself?
  • Will I work with you directly, or with an assistant?
  • How do you handle survey findings?
  • Who holds the deposit, and in what kind of account?
